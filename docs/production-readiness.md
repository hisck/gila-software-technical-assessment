# Production readiness

Fieldwork Market is a local e-commerce implementation for an evaluator running Docker Compose. It covers product administration, CSV import, search, and a complete simulated purchase. This review also evaluates what would change for a public storefront with multiple customers, real payments, and operational commitments. Traffic, recovery targets, sales markets, and fulfillment rules would have to come from that business.

## Current gaps for a public launch

### Access and deployment security

1. **Orders and catalog administration share local access.** The gateway routes the public browsing API, catalog mutations, and the full order list through the same local entry point. Orders have no caller identity or ownership check, and catalog writes have no operator role. Before public exposure, authorize each action in its owning service, scope order queries and payment actions to the customer, and audit privileged writes. Protect session-based mutations from cross-site requests and set per-actor or per-route abuse limits, especially for search, imports, and draft creation. [Gateway routes](../infra/nginx.conf) · [Orders routes](../backend/internal/orders/orders.go) · [Order history query](../backend/internal/orders/history.go) · [OWASP ASVS](https://owasp.org/projects/asvs/)
2. **HTTPS browser writes would fail with the current origin check.** Nginx accepts local hostnames over loopback HTTP. Even after configuring an HTTPS edge and a public hostname, a same-origin browser write carrying an HTTPS `Origin` would be compared with an origin built using `http://` and could return 403. Configure the allowed public origin explicitly, establish TLS at the edge, and verify writes through that deployed route. The Compose stack also uses demo passwords, an AMQP login shared by all services, and unencrypted internal links. Provision rotated secrets, service-specific permissions, network isolation, and transport encryption appropriate to the deployment. [HTTP origin guard](../backend/internal/platform/service.go) · [Nginx](../infra/nginx.conf) · [Compose configuration](../compose.yaml)

### Checkout, payments, and inventory

3. **Payment decisions come from the simulator.** Orders receives the selected outcome from the browser. Payments persists one simulated decision per order and handles repeats through the database and messaging flow. A real integration needs server-created payment sessions, an authoritative amount and currency, provider-side idempotency, verified callbacks, and handling of duplicate or out-of-order events and unknown outcomes. Define how to reconcile provider transactions and settlements with orders, including authorization, capture, voids, refunds, and disputes required by the chosen provider. [Payment submission](../backend/internal/orders/payment.go) · [Payments consumer](../backend/internal/payments/payments.go) · [Example provider webhook guidance](https://docs.stripe.com/webhooks) · [Example provider idempotency guidance](https://docs.stripe.com/api/idempotent_requests)
4. **A stuck checkout can keep stock reserved.** Drafts hold no stock. Once a reservation is held, a failure that leaves a payment or settlement message unprocessed can keep it held indefinitely. Orders can also report `paid` before Catalog processes the inventory commit. Define deadlines, terminal state and settlement semantics, alerts, and safe recovery for overdue drafts and reservations. Check payment state before releasing stock when an authorization or capture result is uncertain. Reconcile paid orders, reservations, and payment records and route discrepancies to an operator. [Inventory reservation](../backend/internal/catalog/inventory.go) · [Orders transitions](../backend/internal/orders/saga.go) · [Manual message replay](../README.md#operations-and-verification)

### Availability and recovery

5. **The local topology has single points of failure.** One gateway, one PostgreSQL server with separate service databases, and one RabbitMQ node share the Compose host. Durable volumes and quorum queue settings help local restarts. A host, database, or broker failure can still interrupt checkout and other services. Design replicas and failover around the required availability, including broker members on independent failure domains. Redis is optional for correctness. Capacity planning must include PostgreSQL load when the cache is cold or offline. [Compose topology](../compose.yaml) · [Service connection pool](../backend/internal/platform/service.go) · [RabbitMQ quorum queues](https://www.rabbitmq.com/docs/quorum-queues)
6. **Message processing can stall and needs operational recovery.** Each publisher takes one outbox row and waits for broker confirmation while holding its transaction. On a single publisher, a persistent publish failure on the oldest row can delay later messages. Consumers handle one delivery at a time per instance. Repeated failures can reach a dead-letter queue and require manual replay. Add age and retry visibility, a deliberate policy for isolating failed messages with ordering constraints, and controlled replay that preserves message IDs. Test backlogs, poison messages, restart, and recovery at the expected load. [Publisher and consumer](../backend/internal/platform/broker.go) · [Current replay instructions](../README.md#operations-and-verification)
7. **There is no automated disaster-recovery or projection rebuild workflow.** Outbox entries are deleted after confirmed publication. If Search loses its projection after the queue drains, the normal delivery path does not repopulate it. Define the accepted loss and recovery times, schedule backups and point-in-time recovery where required, and rehearse coordinated restore across service databases and messaging. Provide a repeatable rebuild from Catalog that accounts for writes and deletes during the rebuild. Verify broker and database recovery against order, payment, and inventory balances. [Outbox deletion](../backend/internal/platform/broker.go) · [Search projection](../backend/internal/search/search.go) · [Disaster recovery guidance](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/framework/rel13.html)
8. **Health checks do not measure progress.** A backend reports healthy when its database responds, even if the broker is unavailable or checkout has stopped progressing. Logs and manual queue inspection are available. Before launch, define service objectives for search freshness, checkout completion, availability, and latency. Track the oldest pending order and outbox row, queue age and dead letters, database saturation, and payment discrepancies. Correlate activity across services and assign alerts and runbooks to an on-call owner. [Health handler](../backend/internal/platform/service.go) · [Current operations guide](../README.md#operations-and-verification)

### Scale, releases, and data governance

9. **Search and history have unmeasured capacity.** Search uses substring predicates, exact counts, offset pagination, and a projection-wide revision. Every cache hit still reads the revision from PostgreSQL. A catalog change makes all prior-revision entries ineligible for cache hits, so frequent changes can reduce hit rates. Order history also counts rows and uses offset pagination. Measure real data volume, concurrent checkouts, status polling, large imports, and degraded dependencies before choosing narrower cache invalidation, indexed search, cursor pagination, or additional workers. Check database connection budgets as replicas increase. [Search queries and cache](../backend/internal/search/search.go) · [Order history query](../backend/internal/orders/history.go) · [Buyer status polling](../frontend/src/checkout.ts) · [Reliability testing guidance](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/wellarchitected/latest/framework/rel12.html)
10. **Safe independent releases need more tooling.** Services run schema DDL at startup, and Go messages use shared structs and versioned routing names. The repo has no versioned migration workflow or automated compatibility gate for schema, event, and HTTP changes. Make migrations explicit, verify compatibility between old and new service versions, and rehearse staged deploys and rollback. Successful JSON API responses are cast to TypeScript types. Add runtime validation where a version mismatch would break critical UI and a regression test that detects incompatible responses. [Schema initialization](../backend/internal/platform/service.go) · [Message contracts](../backend/internal/contract/contract.go) · [Frontend API client](../frontend/src/api.ts)
11. **Verification is local and evidence remains bounded.** The repo supplies Go tests and a Docker smoke suite. Frontend scripts cover development and build, with no maintained frontend lint or browser test command or checked-in CI workflow. Automate the existing checks, add focused browser and contract tests for critical flows, and include dependency and container scans and update policy in CI. Verify accessibility, load, failover, and restore against the release targets. [Frontend scripts](../frontend/package.json) · [Go state model](../backend/internal/orders/saga_model_test.go) · [Smoke suite](../scripts/smoke.py)
12. **Retention and data access policies remain undefined.** Order drafts, processed message IDs, and deleted product markers have no managed retention policy. Growth affects storage, and deleting records carelessly can let old messages act again. Align cleanup with the retry and replay window, preserve required order and financial records, and define limits on access and log contents. Once customer data is collected, establish privacy controls, export and deletion workflows, and policy for backups and audit records based on the markets served. [Delivery tables](../backend/internal/platform/service.go) · [Orders schema](../backend/internal/orders/orders.go) · [Search deletion markers](../backend/internal/search/search.go)

## Commerce capabilities outside the exercise

| Area | Scope to define with the business |
| --- | --- |
| Catalog and merchandising | Variants, images and media, product attributes, merchandising rules, localized content, and inventory across facilities. The exercise uses the provided flat CSV schema. Large or recurring feeds would also need import jobs, progress, traceability, and rules for updating existing SKUs. |
| Discovery | Relevance tuning, typo tolerance, autocomplete, synonyms, richer facets, and search analytics. The current search offers substring matching, category filtering, and paging. |
| Customer and storefront | Accounts or a secure guest checkout, cart continuity across devices, contact details and addresses, order communications, customer support tools, and accessibility and browser coverage. A public catalog may also need crawlable product pages and product metadata. |
| Pricing and finance | Currencies, promotions, tax and exemption rules, invoices, settlement reporting, and accounting integration as required. The current total uses item price and quantity in USD. |
| Stock and fulfillment | Warehouse allocation and stock movements, shipping options and rates, delivery tracking, pickup or backorders where applicable, and exception handling for partial shipments. |
| After-sales | Cancellations, returns, exchanges, refunds, chargebacks, and customer-service workflows with an auditable history. |

The first production milestones are secure customer and operator access, recoverable payment and inventory processing, and a deployment that meets measured availability and recovery targets. Choose the commerce scope for the intended customers and markets before committing to a launch. [Architecture guide](architecture.md)

The [local verification suite](../README.md#operations-and-verification) supplies evidence for the challenge flows. Production throughput, recovery times, dependency advisories, penetration testing, live-provider behavior, and failover require their own validation.
